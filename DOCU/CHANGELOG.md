## 2026-09-21

- Taught the Finance backend the three company answers — **UNIQUE, ELITE and GENERAL** — with the first Finance database change since `finance_v1`. Migration `20260921090000_finance_company_classification` adds **nullable** `ManualExpense.companyId` and `FinancialLedgerEntry.companyId`, both `onDelete: Restrict`, indexed (`manual_expenses.companyId`, and `financial_ledger_entries(companyId, occurredAt)` because every company-scoped aggregate filters company and period together). **GENERAL is not a company**: it is `companyId IS NULL`, the classification of a financial record with no authoritative company-bearing source behind it, such as office rent booked without a Vehicle. `OperatingCompany` still holds exactly two rows, the migration itself aborts if it ever finds a third, and the DTO returns `company: null` rather than a synthetic `{ code: "GENERAL" }` — so the columns must stay nullable and forcing them NOT NULL would mean inventing a company for every general expense. **Manual Expense gains no company selector.** Staff already choose the optional Vehicle and the Vehicle already knows its company, so asking again would only create a way for the two to disagree: create with a Vehicle derives `Vehicle.companyId`, create without one stores `null`, correcting to a different Vehicle re-derives, removing the Vehicle makes it GENERAL, and adding one classifies it — all atomically with the expense update. `CreateManualExpenseSchema` / `CorrectManualExpenseSchema` carry no `companyId` at all, so Zod strips a client-sent one and a request claiming *Vehicle = ELITE, companyId = UNIQUE* still stores ELITE; the Finance Vehicle picker's own company filter stays search UX and never classifies the expense. **The ledger persists its company at write time**, because a recognized movement is history and must never be re-derived from a Vehicle later: Stripe contract payments take the frozen `Contract.companyId` (never the Vehicle's current company), maintenance takes `MaintenanceOrder → Vehicle.companyId`, and the three Manual Expense writers (create, correct reprojection, void reversal) copy `ManualExpense.companyId` verbatim, `null` included. All five funnel through the single `insertLedgerEntry`, which now **requires** `companyId` explicitly, so a future writer cannot silently forget it, and a Manual Expense and its ledger row are written in one transaction and always agree. **Nothing falls back to UNIQUE** — not a writer, not a backfill step, not a query. Finance reads gained a company scope: no parameter is **ALL** (UNIQUE + ELITE + GENERAL), `?companyId=<id>` is that company, `?companyScope=GENERAL` is `companyId IS NULL`, and the two together are refused with 422 `FINANCE_COMPANY_SCOPE_CONFLICT` instead of one silently winning. ALL is deliberately the *absence* of a predicate: written as `companyId IS NOT NULL` it would hide every GENERAL row, which is the one mistake a unit test now pins. Summary, analytics, ledger and open receivables all accept it; ledger aggregates filter the persisted column directly, so a company total needs no join and no per-row lookup. Open receivables derive company through `Contract.company` and gained **no** column — a Contract always has a company, so GENERAL correctly returns none and no "general receivable" concept was invented to fill the gap. The dashboard is untouched: the analytics helpers take an optional scope defaulting to ALL, so `weeklyFinance` behaves exactly as before while Phase C2 can pass a scope without another backend change. The backfill is relation-only — manual expenses from their Vehicle, ledger rows by precedence Contract → Manual Expense → Maintenance → Vehicle → null — with no description parsing and no category guessing, and migration guards that abort on a vehicle-linked expense without a company, on a ledger row disagreeing with its Contract or Manual Expense, or on an unexpected operating company. Counts: `diamond` had 10 manual expenses (all vehicle-less, therefore all GENERAL) and 12 ledger rows (all GENERAL); `haidara_test` had 173 ledger rows, 165 resolved from their Contract, 8 from maintenance → Vehicle, 0 unresolved; zero mismatches on both, and the new read-only `npm run verify:finance-company` reproduces that evidence on any database and refuses to pass on a mismatch or a NOT NULL column.
